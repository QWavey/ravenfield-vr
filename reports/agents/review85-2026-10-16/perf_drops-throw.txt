SCOPE: Dropped weapons, grenade duds and pins, throwing and melee (src/Weapons/WeaponDrops.cs, GrenadeDud.cs, ThrowAndMelee.cs), performance in VR against the 11 ms budget at 90 fps. Steady per-frame cost here is small; the real risk is hitch spikes from synchronous Physics.IgnoreCollision bursts and one per-frame loop of ClosestPoint calls.

  what:   IgnoreBodies (lines 621-625) runs 'if (d.PlayerGeneration != PropPhysics.PlayerGeneration) { ...; PropPhysics.IgnorePlayer(d.Colliders); }' for every drop in the same Update. [major/medium] src/Weapons/WeaponDrops.cs:746  Nearest() calls ClosestPoint on every collider of every drop, both hands, every frame
  what:   Update (lines 580-581) calls Nearest(hand.Palm.position) for every tracked hand with Role Free, every frame, whether or not it is grabbing. Nearest (lines 734-755) runs 'float dist = c != null ? Vector3.Distance(c.ClosestPoint(palm), palm) : float.MaxValue' for every collider of every drop, with no bounds check.
  why:    Per frame, on foot, with drops present: 2 hands x up to 16 drops x 12-24 colliders = 384-768 Collider.ClosestPoint calls on convex MeshColliders, and most drops are metres away. This is the biggest steady per-frame cost in these files. The per-call cost is not measured here (plausibly around 1-2 us), so roughly 0.4-1.5 ms worst case.   change: Before ClosestPoint, test the cheap cached AABB: 'Bounds box = c.bounds; box.Expand(2f * PickupReach); if (!box.Contains(palm)) continue;'. Expand(2 * PickupReach) grows each side by PickupReach, and the AABB contains the shape, so a miss here is a guaranteed miss for ClosestPoint. Nearly all calls disappear when no drop is in reach.
