perf effects (Performance), read-only Haiku agent, 2026-10-16

SCOPE: Read-only performance review of the screen-effects and muzzle-flash files (LensEffects.cs, LensFlash.cs, MuzzleFireball.cs, MuzzleFlashInfo.cs, DamageFeedback.cs, ComfortVignette.cs, MudSplash.cs) against the 11 ms frame budget at 90 fps. Nothing was run or profiled, so ms figures are estimates from the code (counts are exact, costs are rough); WorkMs (LensEffects.cs:127-129) can measure the worker's wall time. Outside performance, noticed in passing: in single-pass stereo the displayed picture is eye 1's (LensEye.cs:1194 shows only the centre quad, which uses _tex[1]), and for a hit from the left HitEye[1] is 0.55 (LensEffects.cs:412), so left-side hits are dimmed in that mode. The user's m4a1 ACOG question is not part of this read-only review.

[major/high] src/Rig/LensEffects.cs:416  Low health keeps the full lens picture recomputed and uploaded every frame
  what:   `any |= j.Hit > 0.001f || j.LowHealth > 0.001f || j.Drops > 0.001f;` makes every frame below half health a full job. RenderBand then runs over all 258k pixels (2 eyes x 4 bands), and the red edge it adds is just `r += LowEdge[i] * low;` (line 703), a static table scaled by one number. Every shown job also runs `_tex[e].SetPixels32(_out[e]); _tex[e].Apply(false, false);` for both eyes on the main thread (lines 347-348). `Array.Clear(acc, ...)` (line 625) runs even in bands where no flash is drawn.
  why:    While health is under half, each frame costs roughly 0.5-1 ms of main-thread time for the two 380x340 RGBA32 uploads (517 KB each, estimate) plus roughly 2 ms of background CPU, for a picture that changes only in brightness. This is the steadiest state in the area, so it is the most frequent per-frame cost.
  change: Take LowHealth out of `any` (line 416) and out of RenderBand (lines 661, 701-704). Draw the red edge on its own quad, following the veil pattern in DamageFeedback.cs:38-39: upload LowEdge (with EdgeFade baked in) once as a texture, and set the quad's tint each frame from the same value passed to SetLowHealth (DamageFeedback.cs:121), keeping the x1 tint convention used for the flash quads. Also determine whether a band has any flash before the Array.Clear at line 625, and skip the clear when it does not.

[major/medium] src/Rig/LensEffects.cs:696  Hit glow evaluates two Gaussians and three tone curves on every pixel of both eyes for 0.5 s per hit
  what:   `float g70 = ExpNeg(q * inv70) * hit, g25 = ExpNeg(q * inv25) * hit;` followed by three `ToneSe(...)` calls (lines 696-699), for every pixel where hit > 0.001 (line 691). The glow's sigmas are about 56 px and 20 px across and 133 px and 47 px down, and ExpNeg only returns 0 past exponent 24, which the picture never reaches, so almost nothing is culled. The shape is identical in both eyes; only `hit` differs (line 650, HitEye).
  why:    About five table exponentials per pixel over 258k pixels, roughly 3-5 ms of CPU per frame for the 0.5 s after each hit (estimate). Combat hits are frequent, so this runs often. Eye 1 recomputes the same q and exponentials that eye 0 already computed.
  change: Compute g70 and g25 (the shape without `hit`) once per frame into shared scratch arrays and reuse them for both eyes, multiplying by each eye's hit strength. Skip pixels whose summed contribution is under half an 8-bit step (exponent above about 6.5). Use the tone lookup from the composition finding above.

[major/medium] src/Rig/LensEffects.cs:813  Full-resolution per-pixel composition of both eyes on four threads for every flash frame
  what:   Every eye picture is CW x CH = 380 x 340 (line 35), 129,200 px, of which 54,200 are the margin beyond the tested 300 x 250 view. For each live flash, LensFlash evaluates every pixel of its band: the tail term, then within the starburst radius 4.5*sStar (sStar = 9 + 22p, about 170 px at the 1.3 power cap, line 25) a FastAtan2 and an ExpNeg (lines 813-838). RenderBand then applies ToneFlash three times and To8 three times per pixel (lines 687-689, 711), each ToneFlash being an ExpNeg (line 1065). That is about 258k pixel evaluations per frame with a flash.
  why:    Roughly 6-10 ms of CPU per firing frame summed over the four threads (estimate, not profiled). That competes with Unity's own threads on a 4-6 core PC, and the threads run at AboveNormal (see the priority finding). Most of the margin is smooth glare that does not need full resolution.
  change: Look-preserving first step: replace the per-channel ToneFlash and ToneSe exponentials (LensEffects.cs:1065-1068) with one lookup table indexed by the accumulated value, step 1/1024 (the curve's slope is at most 1.24, so the error stays under one 8-bit step). Larger steps change the approved pictures, so re-check them with SavePictures: compute the smooth terms (tail, wash, ghosts, veil) at half resolution in the margin, or move the whole composition into a fragment shader on the quad. A custom shader would have to ship in an AssetBundle, since a player build cannot compile shader source at runtime.

[minor/low] src/Rig/LensEffects.cs:202  Worker and band threads run at AboveNormal priority while effects are on
  what:   `Priority = System.Threading.ThreadPriority.AboveNormal` on the worker (line 202) and on the three band threads (line 208). Four threads can be busy at once during flash and hit frames.
  why:    On a 4-6 core PC, four AboveNormal threads can outrank Unity's Normal-priority main and render threads and take CPU time from the game while an effect runs. Not measured. The priority was probably chosen so the picture is not late (comment at lines 200-201), so the trade-off should be measured.
  change: Try Normal priority for the band threads first. If flashes then arrive late, keep AboveNormal only on the worker thread. Measure with WorkMs (lines 127-129) and the frame time.

[minor/high] src/Rig/LensEffects.cs:345  Single-pass stereo computes, uploads and draws an eye-0 picture that is never shown
  what:   StereoMode defaults to MultiPass (src/Core/VRConfig.cs:149), so this only applies when the player picks SinglePassInstanced. Then LensEye.OnWillRenderObject shows only the centre quad (`show = Eye == 2`, LensEffects.cs:1194), whose Picture is `_tex[1]` (lines 182, 195). Even so, Present uploads both textures (lines 347-348), RenderBand runs bands 0 and 1 for eye 0, and quads 0 and 1 stay active and draw black (lines 430-433).
  why:    Half of the background work and one of the two per-frame uploads are wasted in that mode, roughly 0.3-0.5 ms of main-thread time plus about half of the composition cost. Quads 0 and 1 also add two full-view additive passes that contribute nothing.
  change: When LensEye.SinglePass is true, skip eye 0 in the worker (bands 0 and 1, including band 0 run on the worker) and in the upload loop, and call SetActive(false) on quads 0 and 1 in Present (lines 430-433). Keep SavePictures (lines 455-498) working by reading the eye-1 picture for both halves or by adding a flag.

[minor/medium] src/Rig/LensEffects.cs:923  Drop picture rebuilt in full for each eye on every surfacing
  what:   MakeDrops loops `for (int e = 0; e < 2; e++)` (lines 923-928), calling DropField, which clears a 4.65 MB float field (line 935), and ShadeDrops, which clears a 13.9 MB array (line 1005), scans all 1140 x 1020 cells (lines 1008-1036) and downsamples (lines 1038-1059). The two eyes' fields differ only by a 5 px shift (lines 925, 938).
  why:    Roughly 15 ms of worker CPU per surfacing (estimate). It runs before the bands (line 556), so the surfacing frame's picture is late by a couple of frames. It happens once per surfacing, not per frame.
  change: Build the field and shade once, and have eye 1 read the eye-0 result with a 15-column (supersampled) shift, since ShadeDrops depends only on local gradients and is shift-invariant. Clear only the bounding boxes of the drops rather than the whole 1.16 M-cell field before each build.

[minor/high] src/Weapons/LensFlash.cs:110  Muzzle flash info recomputed about five times per shot, with a log message built on every flash-less shot
  what:   `_pendingEveryShot = MuzzleFlashInfo.Of(weapon).Chance >= 0.999f;` (line 110), then `MuzzleFlashInfo.Of(_pendingWeapon).Particles` (line 126), `FlashSize` (line 280, which calls Of internally, called at lines 209 and 214), `MuzzleFlashInfo.Of(w).Particles` (line 214) and `MuzzleFlashInfo.Of(w).Light` (line 245).   why:    Roughly 0.1-0.2 ms of main-thread time and some garbage per shot (estimate). A machine gun fires 10-13 shots per second.

[minor/high] src/Weapons/MuzzleFireball.cs:163  First use of each flash strength and turn builds a texture on the main thread
  what:   TextureFor (lines 155-174) calls LensEffects.Fireball (a 64x64 pass with three exponentials and an atan2 per pixel, LensEffects.cs:722-751), then creates and Applies a Texture2D, the first time each key is needed. Keys are strength tenths (1-16) x eight phase bins, up to 128.
  why:    A hitch of roughly 0.2-0.3 ms on the main thread per new key (estimate), so up to 30-40 ms spread over the first shots of a session.
  change: Build all keys on a background thread at construction, or pre-warm the whole table once at VR start, so no shot waits for a texture.
