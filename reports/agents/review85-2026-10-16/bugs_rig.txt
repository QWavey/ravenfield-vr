[major/high] src/Rig/CockpitControls.cs:164  Turning the view in a car swings the gripped rim and steers the car
  what:   UpdateHand reads the palm in world space (CockpitControls.cs:120) and integrates the rim angle from OnWheelPlane(palm) (lines 164-166). The Anchored branch of PlaceOrigin applies the seat-yaw turn to the rig root (VRRig.cs:845: anchor.rotation *= Quaternion.Euler(0f, _seatYawOffset, 0f)), and VRHand.UpdatePose places every palm through that root (VRHand.cs:96-97: RigidPose rig = RigidPose.Of(rigRoot); Raw = rig.Multiply(rawLocal)). AddYaw adds snap or smooth turns to _seatYawOffset in Anchored mode (VRRig.cs:537-540).
  why:    Cars are not blocked from turning: VehicleInput.BlocksTurning is true only for Helicopter and Airplane (VehicleInput.cs:21; VRRig.cs:493), and the default turn style is Snap (VRConfig.cs:165). A 30 degree snap swings a gripped palm roughly 0.3 m around the eyes, about the size of the rim radius, so the wheel reads tens of degrees of turn per snap. _handSteer clamps at full lock (line 168) and the car keeps steering until the rim is released. Smooth turning does the same continuously while the stick is held.
  change: In VRRig, store the seat yaw that the last PlaceOrigin applied (set a field in the Anchored case near VRRig.cs:845) and add internal Vector3 CockpitPalm(Vector3 palm) that rotates palm about Head.position by minus that yaw around the anchor's up axis. In CockpitControls.UpdateHand use _rig.CockpitPalm(hand.Palm.position) at line 120 for both the wheel and the stick, so the view turn is undone before the rim or stick is read. Recenter() (VRRig.cs:663) also zeroes the offset in one step, so the stored value must be the one used for placement. Add a SelfCheck case that turns the rig about the head with the hand held still: the current SelfCheck (lines 224-252) only moves the hand with the seat, so it cannot see this.

[major/high] src/Rig/CockpitControls.cs:208  Level-horizon option makes aircraft bank read as stick input
  what:   InSeat uses the seat's full rotation: InFrame(_seat.transform, world) (lines 208-211), and the stick throw is the seat-frame displacement of the hand since the grab (lines 182-186). With LevelHorizonInVehicles on, AnchorPose levels the rig root to yaw only (VRRig.cs:915-922, Level), so the hands do not bank while the seat does. OnWheelPlane also uses the wheel's banked transform (line 264).
  why:    A hand 0.3 m from the seat pivot moves about 5 cm in seat space for a 10 degree bank, about two thirds of the 8 cm StickThrow (line 16), so the aircraft's own attitude becomes stick input. The wheel has the same problem: a 10 degree roll turns the rim by 10 degrees against level hands, about a fifth of lock at the default SteeringWheel.maxRotation of 45. The option is off by default (VRConfig.cs:184), so this only affects players who enable it.
  change: For the stick, measure in the rig root: InFrame(_rig.Root, palm) at line 208 (and the same frame for the grab start at line 137), which carries no bank in this mode and also ignores the seat-yaw turn. For the wheel, refuse new cockpit grabs while SmoothVehicleHorizon is on, or keep the hands banked with the seat; a yaw-only frame cannot follow a rim that rolls with the car.

[minor/low] src/Rig/IntroStage.cs:177  Intro walkers are staged against a different camera than the one the headset follows
  what:   Turn() stages the walkers from GameCamera() (line 177), the first enabled non-render-texture camera in Camera.allCameras that is not under the rig (lines 327-338). The headset is placed from GameContext.GameMainCamera through FollowTarget (VRRig.cs:407) and TryGetAnchor(following) (IntroStage.cs:344).
  why:    If the Splash scene has more than one such camera, the walkers are turned toward a different eye point and heading than the one the player sees, so they can pass behind the view or off to one side. The enumeration order of Camera.allCameras is not controlled by the code.
  change: Use the camera the rig follows: pass GameContext.GameMainCamera (or the camera returned by FollowTarget) into Turn and PlaceTitleInWorld instead of GameCamera(). Confirm which cameras exist in Splash with the -rfvr-introdump log, which already lists them.

  what:   Calibration starts only when VRConfig.EyeHeight.Value <= 0f (VRRig.cs:475), but the EyeHeight getter treats any value of 0.3 or less as uncalibrated (VRRig.cs:676), and physical crouch uses the same 0.3 threshold (line 690). VRConfig.EyeHeight has no range (VRConfig.cs:180).
