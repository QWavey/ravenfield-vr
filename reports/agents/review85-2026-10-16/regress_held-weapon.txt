[major/medium] src/Weapons/HeldWeapon.cs:660  Muzzle dip follows body motion: jumps and falls now pitch a one-handed heavy gun
  what:   Line 660: `float ap = -wr * wr * _lagPitch - 2f * zeta * wr * _lagPitchVel + wr * wr * _inertiaLever * (supported ? 0.3f : 1f) * Vector3.Dot(_handAcc, Vector3.up);`. The `wr * wr` factor came in with d5a1423 (blame), so the forcing is now full size; before it the steady dip was about 0.006 deg (the comment at 658-659 says so). `_handAcc` (lines 643-646) is the lerped world acceleration of `main.Velocity` (line 636). VRHand.cs:124 sets `Velocity = hand velocity + rigVelocity`, and rigVelocity (VRHand.cs:112-120) is the rig root's own frame-to-frame motion.
  why:    The doc comment at 622-625 says the weapon is driven by the hand's acceleration only, so a steady hand has it exactly in place. Body acceleration is included, though. The steady state of this spring is `_lagPitch = _inertiaLever * Dot(_handAcc, up)` in degrees. During a jump take-off, a landing, or a free fall (-9.8 m/s² on the body), a one-handed rifle (lever 0.35) pitches about 3.4 deg, a launcher (0.6) about 5.9 deg, a machine gun (0.7) about 6.9 deg, clamped at 10 deg, for the whole air time. Two-handed (factor 0.3) it is about 1 to 2 deg. The same pitch moves the muzzle pose used by the aim tests at lines 824 and 842, so a jump can also drop aim. The position lag already follows body acceleration (pre-existing, 2 to 8 cm), so only the new rotational term is unintended.
  change: Drive only the pitch term from the hand's own acceleration. In VRHand.cs add `public Vector3 BodyVelocity { get; private set; }` and set it next to line 124 as `Velocity - rigVelocity` (the value already stored at line 127). In HeldWeapon.StepInertia keep a second accumulator fed from `main.BodyVelocity` with the same lerp as line 646, and use it only in line 660. Leave `_lagPos` on `_handAcc` unless position should also ignore locomotion. Optionally lower the +/-10 deg clamp at line 669.
