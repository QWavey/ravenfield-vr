[major/medium] src/Weapons/BodyZones.cs:32  Holster hit spheres overlap: belt gear and pouch share the same area
  what:   `public const float HolsterRadius = 0.2f;` (line 32) applies to every spot (HolsterAt 248, HolsterNearerThanPouch 431, DrawDebug 504). In the Boneworks layout `DomBeltFront = belt + TorsoRight * (0.09f * side)` and `OffBeltFront = belt - TorsoRight * (0.09f * side)` (116-117) are 0.18 m apart, and the Boneworks pouch `hipSide + TorsoRight * (0.17f * gunSide)` (124, hipSide = belt - TorsoForward*0.1 at 119) is about 0.13 m from DomBeltFront. With 0.20 m holster spheres and a 0.17 m PouchRadius (line 33), those areas overlap, and Check (408-416) gives the item to whichever centre is nearest.
  why:    A hand reaching for a magazine at the front edge of the pouch can pull the belt item, and a hand aimed at a belt item can be counted as the pouch. The wrong thing comes off the belt, and nothing about the hit area depends on what is being carried.
  change: Make the radius per spot through a helper such as SpotRadius(Spot): 0.20 for BackDominant, BackOff, HandgunDominant and HandgunOff (shoulders and underarms), and about 0.12 for the belt-front gear spots (GearDominant, GearOff) and LowerBack, so 0.18 m spacing does not overlap. Pass that radius into Check (408) and HolsterNearerThanPouch (431, which currently uses HolsterRadius). Per carry type (long, hand, gear) is also a reasonable alternative.

  what:   `missing` only tests File.Exists (27-30). The copy loop skips absent sources (`if (File.Exists(src))`, 49-53), yet the method returns the 'Installed ... restart Ravenfield to play in VR' string unconditionally (60).

[minor/low] src/Weapons/BodyZones.cs:58  Torso settles toward the head at 25 deg/s even inside the 35 deg band
  what:   The comment at 58 says 'Torso follows the head only once it has turned past a comfortable neck angle', but line 64 `_torsoYaw = Mathf.MoveTowardsAngle(_torsoYaw, headYaw, 25f * Time.unscaledDeltaTime);` runs every frame, outside the `if (Mathf.Abs(delta) > 35f)` block (60-63).

[minor/medium] src/Weapons/VRScope.cs:262  Lens angle uses a fixed 9 cm eye relief; scope models measure about 7.8 cm
  what:   `float lensAngle = 2f * Mathf.Atan(_radius / EyeRelief) * Mathf.Rad2Deg;` (262) uses the constant `EyeRelief = 0.09f` (17). For analysed scope models the lens sits at `ScopeOcular - dir * 0.002` (55) and the eye point is `ocular - AimAxis * ScopeEyeRelief` with ScopeEyeRelief = 0.08f (WeaponAnalyzer.cs:817, 881).   why:    With a 1.25 cm lens radius the code uses about 15.8 degrees where the real eyepiece subtends about 18.2 degrees, so the magnified picture is roughly 13% more zoomed than the scope it stands in for. The player sees a tighter view than the scope gives.
  change: In the private constructor compute `_relief = Vector3.Distance(eyePoint, lensPos)` and use `_relief` at line 262 instead of EyeRelief. The SightLine constructor already places the lens at EyeRelief, so it stays correct.

[minor/medium] src/Weapons/VRScope.cs:280  Scope eye box is the same for every sight regardless of magnification
  what:   `float push = Mathf.Max(0f, offAxis - 0.008f) / 0.03f;` (280) and `float brightness = Mathf.Clamp01(1f - (offAxis - 0.03f) / 0.04f);` (282) use fixed thresholds. `magnification` (261) only feeds the camera FOV (263).
  why:    A 1.5x magnifier and an 8x scope get the same 3 cm full-picture tolerance and the same 7 cm blackout. Real eye boxes shrink as power rises (exit pupil is roughly objective over magnification), so high-power sights are too forgiving and low-power ones are not forgiving enough. A player scanning through a 1.5x sight feels the same tight window as through a 8x.
  change: Compute `float box = Mathf.Clamp(4f / magnification, 0.6f, 2f);` after line 261 and scale the thresholds: `push = Mathf.Max(0f, offAxis - 0.008f * box) / (0.03f * box)`, and `brightness = Mathf.Clamp01(1f - (offAxis - 0.03f * box) / (0.04f * box))`.
