   src/Rig/CockpitControls.cs:72
   what: _steer is written only while a hand is on the rim (lines 70 and 74) and is reset only in ReleaseAll (line 279, which runs on seat change or menu open).    trigger: Turn the wheel to about half right with one hand and let go; the car straightens from the stick.    fix: In Update(), extend the chain at lines 72-75 with `else { _steer = 0f; }` so a fresh grab starts from centre. The game's SteeringWheel.Update still animates the rim back to zero after release.
   skeptic (correct): REAL, high: Checked src/Rig/CockpitControls.cs and the related code.

2. Regrab path: line 123 sets _wheelBase[i] = _steer (stale, e.g. 0.5). Line 115 computes holding before the grab. 3. Rim: BeforeRender (lines 188-198) writes turn = _steer and euler.z = _steer * maxRotation while a hand is on the rim, so the rim snaps to about half of maxRotation (22.5 deg at the default 45) on grab. Decompiled SteeringWheel.Update (.ref/decompiled/.../SteeringWheel.cs) moves its private turn toward CarInput().x at steerRate 5/s. CarInput is postfixed by VehicleInput.Car() (VehicleInput.cs line 27: WheelSteer ?? left.x), so after release the target is the left stick x (about 0). The rim therefore returns to centre within about 0.1 s from half lock (0.2 s from full lock) and the mod's stored _steer is left at the old angle. The stored state contradicts the visual and car state, which confirms the bug.

4. Other resets: seat change, GameMenuOpen and destroyed vehicle all go through ReleaseAll, which does reset _steer. The second-hand handover (lines 148-153) copies the current _steer, so it is correct. The reported trigger (single-hand release, then regrab in the same seat) is the only path that keeps the stale value, as the finding says.

5. Suggested fix: setting _steer = 0f when no hand is on the rim is safe. BeforeRender returns early in that case and WheelSteer is null, so nothing reads _steer until the next grab. Minor nuance: the game's rim takes up to about 0.2 s to return to zero, so a regrab inside that window still snaps the remaining angle to zero. This is much smaller than the reported jump and does not make the finding false.

3. [CONFIRMED] [minor] Smooth turn is not capped per frame, unlike the HUD update
   src/Rig/VRRig.cs:524
   what: HandleTurning scales the smooth turn by Time.unscaledDeltaTime with no cap (line 524). The HUD follow in BeforeRender clamps the same kind of per-frame motion to 0.1 s (line 768: Mathf.Min(Time.unscaledDeltaTime, 0.1f)). A frame stall therefore turns the view in one step.
   trigger: TurnMode Smooth with the turn stick at full deflection (120 deg/s by default). A 0.2 s frame hitch (explosion, shader compile, streaming) turns the view 24 degrees in a single frame; a longer stall gives a bigger snap.
   fix: Use Mathf.Min(Time.unscaledDeltaTime, 0.1f) at line 524, as the HUD code already does.
   skeptic (correct): REAL, medium: Mechanism confirmed. VRRig.cs:524 computes delta = scaled * SmoothTurnSpeed * Time.unscaledDeltaTime with no cap, and AddYaw (line 543) adds it straight to Yaw. Yaw is consumed uncut in PlaceOrigin (line 813, Quaternion.Euler(0, Yaw, 0) applied to Root), so the rotation lands in the same rendered frame. HandleTurning runs from Update (line 291). Values: stick at full deflection gives scaled = 1.0 (deadzone 0.15, VRConfig.cs:187). Default SmoothTurnSpeed is 120 deg/s (VRConfig.cs:166), range 30 to 360. A 0.2 s stall therefore yields 24 deg in one frame, and the step is linear in the stall length. Line 768 does clamp the HUD call to 0.1 s, as the finding says. Nothing else in the mod clamps this path: grep finds no Time.maximumDeltaTime or Time.timeScale writes in src/, and the 0.2 s figure is far below any Unity clamp anyway. Caveats that weaken the severity: (1) the trigger needs TurnMode=Smooth, but the default is Snap (VRConfig.cs:163); (2) the vignette is driven by stick deflection (line 315), which partly masks the step; (3) the HUD cap at line 768 is a different mechanism (a lazy-follow Slerp step with a separate 35 deg trail limit), so it is not a like-for-like precedent. The finding's stated fix is not neutral: with a 0.1 s cap the integrated turn is under-delivered whenever a frame exceeds 0.1 s (a 0.2 s hitch gives 12 deg instead of 24), so turn rate drops with frame time under load. So the defect is real but minor and a judgement call on whether a one-frame catch-up is a bug.
      correction: Real but minor. The one-frame step is confirmed as described, but the trigger is only TurnMode=Smooth (default is Snap, VRConfig.cs:163), and the suggested fix is a trade-off rather than a clean fix. Capping the integrated delta at 0.1 s bounds each frame to 12 deg at the default 120 deg/s, but it under-delivers turn during any frame longer than 0.1 s, so turn rate becomes frame-rate dependent under load. If the goal is to remove the single-frame step, smooth the applied yaw instead (move an applied yaw toward the commanded yaw at a limited rate, in PlaceOrigin or AddYaw) so the total turn stays time-correct. The HUD cap at line 768 is a different mechanism and is not a like-for-like precedent.
   skeptic (reach): REAL, medium: Confirmed in code. VRRig.cs:524 computes delta = scaled * VRConfig.SmoothTurnSpeed.Value * Time.unscaledDeltaTime with no cap. The default SmoothTurnSpeed is 120 (VRConfig.cs:166) and the settings panel clamps it to 30-360 (VRSettingsPanel.cs:67). At full deflection scaled = 1, so a 0.2 s frame gives 24 degrees. HandleTurning runs from Update (VRRig.cs:291), AddYaw adds to Yaw with no smoothing (VRRig.cs:543), and PlaceOrigin sets Root rotation from Yaw in the same frame's BeforeRender (VRRig.cs:813, 839). So the step reaches the view in one frame. The HUD cap at VRRig.cs:768 is a different mechanism: UpdateHudPose (lines 269-275) is an exponential smoothing filter with a 35 degree clamp, where a large dt would converge in one step. Its presence is not evidence that the turn must also be capped.

Reachability: stalls of 100-300 ms are plausible. Tasks.md:148 documents first-draw hitches, Tasks.md:215 records a 132 ms p99 and 546 ms max, though that was a null-driver run at about 12 fps rather than real play at 45-90 fps. Explosions and shader compiles can also stall frames. The stick being at full deflection during such a stall is an ordinary combat situation.

Where the finding overstates it: the turn is integrated over real elapsed time, so the total rotation equals what the stick commanded over the stall. The 24 degrees is rotation the stall delayed, released in one frame, not extra rotation. The suggested cap would instead drop rotation: a 0.2 s stall with a 0.1 s cap loses 12 degrees permanently. So the fix trades a catch-up step for a rotation deficit. The defect is a minor comfort issue (a one-frame step after a hitch), not an overshoot. I still count it as real, since a 24 degree jump after a stall is visible and reachable, but with medium confidence because it is closer to a tuning trade-off than a clear logic error.
      correction: Reframe the finding: the single-frame step is catch-up for rotation delayed by the stall, not extra rotation, and it is minor. Capping dt at 0.1 s at VRRig.cs:524 would not be a clean improvement: it drops up to 12 degrees of commanded turn for a 0.2 s stall. If the snap should be removed, limit the per-frame yaw rate or spread the pending delta over a few frames, instead of capping dt.
