What is wrong: The timed sections in the autotest table (lines 33-76) add up to about 0.7 ms a frame: UI.Update 0.137, Rig.BeforeRender 0.088, Weapons.BeforeRender 0.077, Input 0.071, Weapons.Update 0.070, UI.BeforeRender 0.057, Rig.Update 0.039, HandModel 2 x 0.029, held-weapon and pouch parts about 0.06 together, and the rest. 2. Skill: OPTIMIZE - UI.Update runs a scene-wide canvas search once a second and allocates about 2 MB on those frames
Complaint: general (frame rate, hitches)
File: src/UI/CanvasManager.cs:127-129 and 199-201 (reached from UISystem.cs:134 via Canvases.Update)
Quote: CanvasManager.cs:128-129: "_nextScan = Time.unscaledTime + (Util.GameContext.InGame && !Util.GameContext.GameMenuOpen ? 1f : 0.4f);" / "Scan();"
Quote: CanvasManager.cs:201: "foreach (Canvas c in UnityEngine.Object.FindObjectsOfType<Canvas>())"
What is wrong: UI.Update is the costliest per-call mod section: 0.137 ms average, 48.30 ms max, 14,437 calls (autotest line 35). Its average allocation is 20,559 bytes per call with 1.0% of calls allocating. By the profile's own numbers that is about 2 MB per allocating call and about 297 MB across the run, roughly 80% of all mod-section allocation (the run had 127 gen-0 collections, line 30). The 1.0% is about 144 calls in 173 s, about one a second, which fits the once-a-second scan in a match. The profile does not split UI.Update, and I did not trace the 2 MB to a line; the scan is the leading candidate, not a proven one.
Trigger / what the player sees: In a match, once a second, the frame that runs the scan carries the search and the allocation. The garbage feeds the gen-0 collections, and the 99th-percentile frame (50.61 ms, line 29) can include such frames. The steady part is at most 0.137 ms a frame.
Proposed change: First time Scan() on its own with PerfProbe.Part, and the rest of UISystem.Update separately, so the next profile names the source. If the scan is the cost: keep the full FindObjectsOfType only at scene change and when a menu opens, and between those look only at the canvases already tracked or at Canvas.GetCanvasList. Check the Unity 2020.3 behaviour of GetCanvasList before relying on it: if it returns active canvases only, a switched-off menu is found later than it is now, so the full search has to stay on menu open.
Estimated saving: upper bound 0.137 ms a frame (the whole UI.Update average), plus one allocation spike a second. If the 2 MB is the scan, up to about 80% of the mod's per-frame garbage. Not measured.
Confidence: medium that UI.Update is the allocating part (the profile says so); low that the scan is the cause (no split in the profile).

3. Skill: OPTIMIZE - the magnified scope lens draws most of the scene a second time on every aimed frame
Complaint: general (frame rate while aiming)
File: src/Weapons/VRScope.cs:344-354
Quote: VRScope.cs:345: "bool render = brightness > 0.004f;"
Quote: VRScope.cs:352: "render &= (Time.frameCount & 1) == 0; // the last picture stays on the lens in between"
Quote: VRScope.cs:354: "_camera.enabled = render;"
What is wrong: While a magnified sight is raised and the eye is within about 7 cm of its line, the lens camera renders most of the scene into a 768 px target with MSAA 2 on every frame. That is a second cull and draw-call submission on the main thread, on top of the two eye passes. The code's own bench note (VRScope.cs:355-357) reads "14.2 ms frames aimed through the RECON LRR's lens, 11.3 without its shadows, 10.8 with no lens at all". So with the lens's shadows off (the default, VRScope.cs:358) the lens costs about 0.5 ms a frame; the other 3.4 ms in that note is its shadow cascades. The every-other-frame option already exists as the BenchMode 2 branch (VRScope.cs:350-352). Tasks.md:1374 keeps the choice open for a headset check.
Trigger / what the player sees: Any magnified sight raised to the eye. Those are the frames a half-rate drop would show on.
Proposed change: Run the bench with BenchMode 2 (every other frame) against the default on a magnified sight, check the picture in the headset as Tasks.md:1374 asks, and keep it only if the picture holds. The note's numbers were frame times, so re-measure the main-thread part too.
Estimated saving: about 0.5 ms a frame while an aimed lens is up (from the code's own bench note, shadows off); every other frame would take about half of that.
Confidence: medium. The figure comes from a comment in the code, not from this sweep; the code path is verified.

4. Skill: OPTIMIZE - late shape fits cook up to three convex hulls on the main thread every frame
Complaint: general (hitch; once per weapon type)
File: src/Weapons/WeaponDrops.Spawning.cs:1299-1302; src/Weapons/ShapeFit.cs:263-281; called every frame from src/Weapons/WeaponDrops.Pickup.cs:24 (UpdateLateFits) through WeaponSystem.cs:190 (Drops.Update)
Quote: WeaponDrops.Spawning.cs:1299-1300: "int cooked = 0;" / "for (; fit.Baked < fit.Pieces.Length && cooked < 3; fit.Baked++, cooked++)"
Quote: ShapeFit.cs:273: "Physics.BakeMesh(p.Mesh.GetInstanceID(), true);"
What is wrong: The fit itself runs on the thread pool (WeaponDrops.Spawning.cs:1358-1369, FitCloud is pure arithmetic per ShapeFit.cs:252-256), but every hull is cooked on the main thread by Physics.BakeMesh, three pieces per frame, until the fit's up to 12 pieces are cooked (FitCloud call with maxPieces 12 at WeaponDrops.Spawning.cs:1362). Each cook is a synchronous PhysX call. Nothing times it, and the per-frame limit is a count, not a time, so a frame with three large hulls pays for three cooks.
Trigger / what the player sees: The first drop of each weapon type that needs a late fit: about four frames of cooking for that type. It is a hitch, not the steady half rate, and it does not repeat once the type is stored (ShapeFit.Store, WeaponDrops.Spawning.cs:1307, and the Cache lookup at ShapeFit.cs:205).
Proposed change: Budget the cooks by time instead of count (stop the loop after about 1 ms of cooking in a frame and resume next frame), and time ShapeFit.Bake with PerfProbe so the cost of one cook is known before the budget is chosen.
Estimated saving: not measured here. The three-per-frame bound is verified; the time per cook is not.
Confidence: low (the cook count is verified from the code; the cook time is not).
